# Silas Ashwood

[[maren-ashwood]]'s younger brother. Left Briarhollow eleven years ago after their parents' burying and settled in [[ravencraw|RavenCraw]], a small town several days' journey north, without ever sending word home. Has not appeared in person; everything known of him so far comes through [[player|Christopher]], whom he sent to Briarhollow.

He is unwell — afflicted, by his own belief, with something evil centered on the [[small-green-book|small green book]] that was among their late father's things. He lodges at the [[slippery-gypsy-inn|Slippery Gypsy Inn]] in RavenCraw, where innkeeper [[karl]] reports he hadn't left his room in about three days: no meals, no sign of him leaving even for the privy. Karl knocked twice — turned away the first time, and the second time heard him through the door arguing with someone, though no one else was in the room.

Met [[player|Christopher]] face to face for the first time on the early afternoon of the 20th of Greenveil, after three days shut in. Gaunt to the point of wasting, cheeks sunken, beard patchy where it looks pulled at, skin an unhealthy candle-wax color. Healing self-inflicted marks along one forearm — the kind made when something won't leave a person alone otherwise. His room bears the evidence: a rough ring scratched into the floorboards around his bed, deep enough to splinter the wood, close to a warding sigil but not quite one; a broken line of salt across the threshold; the room's one mirror turned to the wall and nailed shut that way; days of untouched food gone bad on the floor.

His voice on waking was wrecked, unused-to-gently. His first words to Christopher: relief that he'd come, and that the book's binding held — then a question that suggests the book's presence in Briarhollow never actually stopped it reaching him: *"Is it still talking to you? Because it never stopped talking to me."*

Pressed on it, he describes the entity's voice going quieter — not silent — two or three days after Christopher left Briarhollow with the book, then returning three days ago in a colder, more patient register, as if she'd stopped raging and started calculating. That's when he stopped being able to leave his room. He wants to seek out "the elders" Christopher mentions, and is done being alone with her voice.

When the elders proved a week out, he agreed — warily — to let Christopher use an undisclosed power to skip that week for the two of them, lying still against him for it and swearing not to tell anyone what he saw. Came out the other side of it to find his sister **Maren** in the doorway, alive and well and having crossed the length of the road he never did, eleven years after he walked away from her.
