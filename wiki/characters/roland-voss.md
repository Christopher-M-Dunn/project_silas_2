# Captain Roland Voss

*Revealed: tracked by a beacon signal on the second-in-command's handheld to a wreck valley inland from [[the-terraced-shore]], sighted at range through a gash in the hull, then reunited face to face after dark on Day 3.*

Captain of the [[the-kestrel|*Kestrel*]] and its crew of four. A career surveyor in his fifties, grey-cropped and broad, with the patience of a man who has been wrong exactly often enough to distrust being sure. By-the-book to a degree that maddens his officers and has kept every one of them alive — until, perhaps, now. His dry, buried humor surfaces about once a week, always when it's least expected and most needed.

To his second-in-command he has been mentor, sparring partner, and occasionally a wall: he asks for counterarguments and then argues with them. He does not say what he thinks of people; he assigns them things, and the things say it.

**Fate:** alive, reunited with the second-in-command after dark on Day 3, inside the wrecked *Kestrel*. Leg broken and self-splinted, otherwise holding up on rationed supplies and captain's discipline. Confirmed their identity by demanding a detail only they'd know before he'd let himself believe it, then broke composure just long enough to grip them hard by both arms before reverting to type: "Report" — followed immediately by shoving a canteen at them instead of waiting for one.

Had no idea this world was inhabited — no contact, no sensor readings, nothing in two days alone with a radio that never answered. Took the news of Rusty, the sleek creatures, and the tall watchers hard and fast, cracking his usual flat composure for a second time before locking back down into threat-assessment mode: calls the watchers "disciplined" rather than dangerous, which from him carries its own weight. Can't bear any weight on the broken leg — moving him out tonight, quietly, past a patrol of the watchers that already nearly caught his second-in-command once, isn't happening.

Handed the plan to Kristoff, then talked it through with them anyway: agreed the watchers' eyes and their refusal to reposition after dark both point to night vision, meaning stealth was likely never real. His assessment — patient behavior so far reads as non-hostile, not indifferent — and his call: stop hiding and make the exposure deliberate, the same way Kristoff already did once at the terrace door. Left the actual decision to Kristoff, who proposed staying put — moving him tonight would be noisy and gain nothing if the watchers already see fine in the dark — and instead walking out together, openly, straight from the wreck at first light. Approved it on the spot.

Confirmed his leg: closed break, well-splinted, no fever or infection so far, but cannot bear weight under any circumstances. At dawn, got up on an improvised crutch and walked out of the wreck with Kristoff, openly, hands empty — the watchers shadowed the whole descent without closing in, and held back at the edge of tended ground once the pair reached open shore. First steps he's taken since the crash.

Reached [[the-raft|Rusty]] and watched it grow a ramp on sight, unprompted, to spare his leg the climb — his first unfiltered look at what Kristoff had been describing. Moments later, [[the-sleek-creatures|the sleek creatures]] surfaced in the shallows and fixed on him entirely, reading him the way they'd read Kristoff days before. First contact of his own, mid-composure, hand twitching toward a sidearm he doesn't have. Closed his eyes on Kristoff's advice and let it land — didn't say what he felt, but came out of it visibly less composed than he went in. Called them "not hostile," and admitted, for him a real concession, that it would take some getting used to. Ate Rusty's fruit on Kristoff's word alone, gratefully, and clocked the water content before the taste. Watched Rusty grow an unidentified medicinal-smelling plant near his leg unprompted moments later — tested high in alkaloids, possible neurotoxin. Ruled out eating it: "I'll take the pain over finding out the hard way what a plant's idea of medicine looks like."

When Kristoff voiced the fear that Juno or Sefa might not have survived to reach a beacon at all, didn't deflect it: agreed there's no way to know, said plainly he's "buried that arithmetic before," and set the priority instead on what's actionable — better tools, better map, ready to act the moment either beacon lights up.

Spotted a fixed column of smoke with Kristoff, rising from unexplored high country further inland — noted immediately that nothing they've met so far uses fire, and called it "a third option nobody asked for."

Agreed to a controlled test of Rusty's medicinal plant, on his own terms: smallest possible dose, diluted sap on the tongue rather than swallowed, vitals watched the whole time, stop at the first sign of anything wrong. Ten minutes in, reported real pain relief — "like it's a couple hours further from the break than it actually is" — no fog, no dizziness, only mild numbness at the application site. Revised his assessment on the spot: "Rusty's medicine cabinet just outperformed anything in the pod's kit."

Heard out Kristoff's case for [[human-space|why no rescue is coming]] — no bearing to aim a directed SOS, no power source for a blind subspace broadcast, the *Kestrel* herself never having been the real answer even whole — and didn't argue a point of it, admitting he'd run the same arithmetic himself, second night alone in the wreck. Confirmed standard search protocol exists but on a timescale neither of them expects to matter. Reframed the mission on the spot, without much ceremony: not *get home*, but *live well here, as long as we get to* — a problem he said he could still command.

Went very still — the same stillness as the night he learned about the watchers he'd never detected — when Kristoff recalled the missing Sagittarius A* lock from the ship's last seconds. Admitted he'd run the crash's other impossibilities himself (the lost days, the "survivable" course odds) and deliberately stopped rather than finish the thought. Connected it unprompted to something neither of them had ever remarked on: this world's sky has never once shown a star — floated the guess that the overcast itself might be hiding them.

Corrected on that guess: it's ordinary cloud cover, not something stranger. Took a moment to actually catch up to the real implication — a full radio-source catalog logged automatically during the four unconscious days, cross-referenced against everything the *Kestrel* had on file, matching nothing. Absorbed, without flinching outwardly, that the displacement might be in time as well as space — Sol could be long dead and neither of them would know it. Filed the mystery rather than chase it: agreed with Kristoff's "coincidence is a non-starter" premise about the planet itself, weighed both of Kristoff's theories (deliberate rescue by a local party, or an ancient transit system never built for passengers) without favoring either, and set his working principle plainly: "file the mystery, work the planet" — the only piece of it he says he actually knows how to command.

Granted Kristoff's point that a ship lost surveying the anomaly would draw a specific search there eventually, but downgraded the hope anyway: likely months out, no guarantee of a thorough search rather than a scan-and-log, no guarantee Central risks more ships after losing one already, no guarantee a rescue ship wouldn't share the *Kestrel*'s fate. Filed it "not zero, not soon."

Confirmed he still can't walk — pain relief doesn't fix the break — and agreed to hold position aboard Rusty with the sleek creatures while Kristoff goes ashore alone to recon a new stretch of coast nearer the smoke. Sent them off with a dry "try not to find a second doorway to walk into unannounced."
