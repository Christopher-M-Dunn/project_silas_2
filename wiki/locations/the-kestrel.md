# SV *Kestrel*

A long-range survey vessel, crew of four, years past her refit date and held together by [[juno-park]]'s spite-based maintenance. Cramped, familiar, home: a two-deck spine of lab, galley, engineering, and a cockpit with one cracked seat nobody ever fixed because it was Captain [[roland-voss|Voss]]'s and he said it kept him humble.

Lost in an uncharted region of space — how, exactly, is a question for calmer hours — she came down somewhere on [[the-planet]]. The order to abandon ship came late and loud.

She was surveying a class D spatial anomaly in a sparse stretch of Ring Omicron when whatever happened, happened: four days unaccounted for on the ship's local realtime clock, gravity gone, navigation off the grid entirely, half her systems dark, atmosphere venting — and somehow, out of that, a survivable course toward the one planet in reach, odds that shouldn't exist for a derelict on an uncontrolled vector. In the last seconds before the evacuation order, the nav panel showed no lock on Sagittarius A* at all — not garbled or out of range, simply gone. One scheduled long-range scan completed automatically during the four lost days, while the whole crew was unconscious: zero hits, consistent with what the escape pod's own nav later showed. Neither fact was understood at the time; both were only connected afterward, on [[the-planet]], once it became clear its sky has never shown a single star.

Sighted at range on Day 3: wreckage wedged into a valley slope inland from [[the-terraced-shore]], on the same fixed landmass — hull plating, dulled and split, half swallowed by the rock it struck. Too far yet to confirm survivors, or to be certain it's her and not simply consistent with her.

On the near ridge between the sighting point and the wreck: two more upright, slight figures, the same general build and skin as [[the-terrace-dweller]], crouched still and watching the wreck themselves, facing away, unaware they've been seen. Each carries something slim and dark strapped along a forearm — tool, instrument, or weapon, unconfirmed at range — that the terrace dweller wasn't seen carrying.

Skirted around them undetected (one close call with a loose stone) to a shelf of broken ground right below the wreck. Through a buckled gash in the hull: [[roland-voss|Captain Voss]], alive, propped against a bulkhead, one leg bound in a splint. After dark on Day 3, the second-in-command approached the gash and the two reunited — first crew reunion since the crash. The gash itself is passable but tight and jagged-edged; the deck inside is canted enough that moving an unweight-bearing man out of it quietly, past a patrol of the watchers, is not a one-person or one-night job as things stand. Rather than risk that move in the dark for no real gain, Kristoff and Voss stayed put in the wreck for the night instead. At dawn, salvaged the nav screen and a flare gun, got Voss up on an improvised crutch, and walked out together — openly, hands empty, first steps he's taken since the crash. The watchers shadowed the descent from the ridge without closing in, and stopped at the edge of tended ground once the pair reached open shore, Rusty visible down the coastline.
