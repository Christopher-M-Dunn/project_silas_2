# The Anomaly — HIDDEN

*Unknown to the player. Draft, subject to retcon until narrated. Kristoff and Voss have begun suspecting the shape of this on Day 4; nothing below is confirmed to them yet.*

The *Kestrel* did not crash by any process her instruments could have logged as a crash. The class D spatial anomaly she was surveying in Ring Omicron was not a naturally occurring phenomenon — it is (unknown to any human) one of a scattered, ancient set of transit points, built by no species now living on [Ktarluud](ktarluud.md) or anywhere the Veth archives reach, that intermittently draws in and delivers distant matter to this world. It has done this before, across a span of time none of the three peoples measure in anything shorter than generations. The Veth archives Voss and Kristoff will never see directly hold at least a partial record of it: "things that fall from the sky have fallen before — long ago" (see [The Veth](veth.md)).

What actually happened to the *Kestrel*, mechanically: the anomaly does not transport across ordinary distance. The four lost days on the local realtime clock, the dead navigation, the venting atmosphere, and the sourceless "survivable" approach vector are all downstream of the same event — the ship was pulled out of normal space entirely and returned to it already falling toward Ktarluud, displaced by something no distance-based measurement the crew has can describe correctly.

**The sky is ordinary cloud cover — the mystery is what's above it.** One scheduled long-range scan completed automatically during the four lost days, while the whole crew was unconscious: it caught a real catalog of radio sources straight through the overcast, cross-referenced them against everything aboard, and matched none of them — not Sagittarius A*, not Sol, not one star or pulsar on file. The pod's own nav shows the same zero. The stars up there are real. They are simply not any stars human charts have ever catalogued — consistent with a displacement so total that "further out than Ring Omicron" undersells it: a different, unmapped region of the same galaxy at minimum; possibly a different galaxy; possibly a different era of the same galaxy, Sol included, if enough time passed in transit that nothing lines up anymore. Which of these is true is not fixed — draft space, to be decided when play presses on it.

Kristoff has independently floated two theories for how the *Kestrel* ended up on Ktarluud specifically, given the odds against a "survivable collision course" being coincidence: (1) something on this world brought them here on purpose, or (2) the *Kestrel* passed through an ancient transit system never built with organic passengers in mind, and was carried and expelled the way cargo survives a wreck the crew doesn't — which would also explain four days lost and every survivor's clock resetting to the same instant. Theory 2 is close to the actual truth above. Neither theory is confirmed to the crew, and neither should be, until play actually reveals it.

**Disposition:** none of the three native peoples caused this or fully understand it either; it is older than the Veth's own written record. Nobody on Ktarluud is currently doing anything about the *Kestrel* specifically because of it.
