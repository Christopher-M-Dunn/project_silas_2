# Dr. Sefa Naim — HIDDEN

*Fate after the evacuation. Draft, subject to retcon until narrated.*

Sefa's pod ejected clean but shed its chute early, and came down shattered on the back of a [[ktar|Ktar]] — a big, old, patient one. Sefa is hurt: ribs, collarbone, a concussion that comes and goes. They are also, though they have not fully understood it yet, being *nursed*. The garden around the wreck of their pod has been quietly rearranging itself — windbreak first, then soft ground, then water-gourds and split fruit appearing each amber morning within arm's reach, each one gentler on a battered human stomach than the last, as the Ktar reads what gets eaten and refines its table.

Sefa, concussed or not, is a scientist to the bone: their longhand journal already contains the sentence *"the island is feeding me deliberately"* — underlined twice — and margin-sketches of every gift. Of all the crew, Sefa is closest to grasping what the locals actually are.

**Day 1, through first dark:** in and out of consciousness beside the broken pod. Between wakings, the ground under them softened by degrees and a windbreak of broad leaves thickened against the night; they woke once in the dark to find a water-gourd standing within reach of their good arm that had not been there before, drank it, and managed one journal line by feel before going under again: *"the island is feeding me deliberately"* — underlined twice.

**Day 2, dawn through second dusk:** the concussion loosened its grip by degrees through the daylight hours — longer stretches awake, less doubling of the light. Ribs and collarbone still stop most movement before it starts, but they managed to prop themselves up against the pod's shell by midday. Ran the first deliberate test of the hypothesis: said "thank you," aloud, to the open air after a fruit appeared, and watched — the next offering, hours later, came already split open along the exact line their weak hand would need to eat it one-handed. Wrote four more journal lines, all underlined, one of them just *"it is listening."* Slept again through the worst heat of the afternoon; woke at second dusk clearer-headed than at any point since the crash.

**Second dark, to Day 3's dawn:** slept in longer stretches than any night since the crash, waking only twice — once to drink, once because the windbreak had thickened further of its own accord and the change in airflow woke them. Tried the second deliberate signal at the last waking: a slow, open-palmed pass over the nearest leaf-cluster, the closest they could manage one-handed to a bow. No visible response before sleep took them again, but they noted it anyway, underlined, to check at dawn. Woke at first light stiff but genuinely clear-headed for the first time since the crash.

**Through Day 3's daylight and dark, to Day 4's dawn:** the open-palmed pass got its answer by midday — a new growth, unlike anything offered before, unfurled directly over their resting spot: broad, layered leaves angled to break the worst of the light, a shelter rather than a meal. Logged it as the clearest two-way exchange yet, gesture for gesture. Stood, briefly, for the first time, with help from the pod's frame — ribs protested but held. Slept through the second night properly, waking clear-headed and, for the first time, hungry in a way that felt like recovery rather than survival.
