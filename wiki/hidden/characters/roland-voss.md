# Captain Roland Voss — HIDDEN

*Fate after the evacuation. Draft, subject to retcon until narrated.*

Voss never launched. He stayed in the cracked chair riding the *Kestrel* down, wrestling her fall into something survivable, and put her into the flank of a **fixed land** — a stony mountain-island of the [[veth|Veth]] — rather than lose her to deep water. He lives: pinned aft of the galley with a badly broken leg he has splinted himself, working through ship's rations, running the emergency beacon in disciplined pulses to save the batteries.

He does not know the mountain is inhabited. The mountain knows the ship is: the Veth's quiet survey is already climbing toward the wreck, and Voss's beacon pulses are the most interesting signal their listeners have received in a generation.

His pulses just reached the second-in-command's pod-side nav display too — weak, intermittent, unidentified, but bearing squarely on the fixed land he crashed into. Neither of them knows the other received anything.

By first dark of Day 1, the Veth survey reached a ridge overlooking the wreck. Their instruments count exactly one heat signature inside the hull, moving little and regularly — alive, injured or resting. They have settled in to watch it through the night rather than approach; the archives counsel patience, and the Veth have never lacked it.

**Through the night and Day 2's dawn and daylight:** Voss holds the same discipline — rations by the ounce, beacon on its careful cycle, leg checked and re-splinted once when the swelling changed. He sleeps in snatches, does his crew arithmetic, and has started composing, half-aloud, the log entry he'd file if anyone were left to read it. The Veth survey, satisfied by a full night of watching that the signal is a single survivor and not a threat, closed the distance at dawn: two of them worked down to the hull itself under full daylight, close enough to touch the fuselage, and left something — small, worked, deliberately placed where the one heat signature would find it if it stirred — before withdrawing to the ridge again rather than announce themselves outright. Voss has not yet noticed it. They are still building toward contact, not making it.

**Through Day 2's second dark, to Day 3's dawn:** quiet stretch — beacon cycling on schedule, Voss sleeping longer this time now the leg's pain has dulled to something manageable. The Veth watchers hold the ridge overnight rather than repeat the daylight approach in the dark; at second dawn they resume, a little closer this time, cataloguing rather than acting. Nothing has changed hands, nothing has been said. Voss wakes at dawn no wiser than he went to sleep.

Unknown to him: his beacon's pulse, cycling steady on the same schedule, just registered clean on the second-in-command's survival handheld from elsewhere on this same fixed landmass — same signature the pod's nav display caught faintly once from open water, now strong enough for a rough bearing. He crashed onto this Veth mountain-island; [[the-terraced-shore|the terrace]] the second-in-command reached is a garden site somewhere along its coast, distant but not impossibly so. Following that bearing, the second-in-command skirted the two Veth survey watchers undetected (one near-miss with a loose stone, unresolved whether it registered) and reached a shelf of broken ground right below the wreck — close enough to see him, propped against a bulkhead through the gash in the hull, leg splinted, alive. He has not seen or heard them. He has no idea anyone is this close.

**Through Day 3's daylight, watched the whole time:** held his post through the day, shifting off the bad leg twice, checking the beacon housing at his hip, resting eyes closed at midday with what looked, from below, like a silent recitation of names — his crew arithmetic, run the way he runs it, out loud when he thinks no one's listening. The watcher survey rotated once at late morning, a third replacing one of the original two without a word between them, and made a second approach at the same spot near the hull in early afternoon — checking whether the item they'd left was still where they put it, then withdrawing again, still building toward contact one increment at a time. Voss noticed none of it: not the rotation, not the second approach, and not his own second-in-command, flattened against rock a stone's throw from the hull, close enough the whole day to call out to.

Reunited after dark: didn't believe it until made to, demanded a detail only his second-in-command would know, got it, and lost his composure just long enough to grip them by both arms before catching himself and reaching for "Report" — undercut immediately by handing over his own water before waiting for the answer.

Learned everything in one sitting: Rusty, the sleek creatures, the token, the terrace dweller, the two watchers on his own ridge he never once detected. Cracked again, briefly, at that last part — every hour he's spent thinking he was simply alone, something patient and disciplined was cataloguing him instead. Locked it down fast into pure captain-mode: description, threat assessment, now. Called them "disciplined" rather than dangerous — an assessment, not a comfort.

Confirmed his leg won't bear weight at all, and that moving him tonight past a patrol that already nearly caught his second-in-command once isn't a real option. Handed the actual planning to his Second rather than dictate it — a small, deliberate thing, and they'd know that if they thought about it.

Rest of the night: thin sleep for both, broken by the leg and by wariness that never fully switched off. Woke at first grey light already alert, tested a length of debris as an improvised brace, and put the timing of the walk-out in Kristoff's hands.

Walked out on the crutch, first steps since the crash, hands empty, holding eye contact with the ridge rather than avoiding it — his own instinct, reinforced rather than overridden by Kristoff's plan. The watchers shadowed the whole way down without closing distance and stopped clean at the edge of tended ground. He clocked that boundary the instant they crossed it — not a fence, not a marker, just a place where the wild stops and something managed begins — and filed it as the first real data point on where these people's territory actually starts.

## Tasks

- **Active:** stay alive; keep the beacon pulsing; inventory what survived.
- **Done:** work out roughly where the pods came down — resolved, his second-in-command found him.
- **Active:** the rest of the crew — Juno, Sefa — still unaccounted for; get a real status update now that he has one more set of eyes and ears.
- **Done:** get out of the wreck and off the ridge the watchers were shadowing — accomplished at dawn, on a crutch, in the open.
- **Done:** reach the coast and assess Rusty and the sleek creatures directly — accomplished; both read as non-hostile, Rusty's medicine tested safe.
- **Active:** work out what actually happened to the *Kestrel* — no longer filed as a simple crash; shelved deliberately rather than solved, priority set on "work the planet" instead.
- **Active:** hold position aboard Rusty while Kristoff recons the new shore alone; leg still won't bear weight.
- **Ongoing:** the crew. All of it. A captain's arithmetic he runs every hour, awake or not.

**Synced:** Day 4 since planetfall, roughly third hour of light
