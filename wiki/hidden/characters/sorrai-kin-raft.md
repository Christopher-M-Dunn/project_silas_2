# A Sorrai Kin-Raft — HIDDEN

*Unknown to the player. Draft, subject to retcon until narrated.*

The [[sorrai|Sorrai]] kin-raft that felt the pod's descent as one of four points of animal terror streaking down the dark. They have been arguing since, in their wordless way, about whether the feeling was worth chasing — not the wreckage, which means nothing to them, but the mind. They resolved it in favor of going.

Because Sorrai track feeling, not position, their heading has followed the player, not the pod: when the frightened mind moved from the water to the [[first-ktar|raft]], their course corrected without any of them quite noticing they'd done it. They are still distant — far enough that from raft height, in the right light, they read as no more than a brief unnatural line on the water, there and gone. They have no way to know they were seen at all.

Their pace is unhurried but steady; a kin-raft does not rush toward an unknown feeling, but it does not stop, either. The frightened mind's raft has now turned and is moving toward Veth shore under its own power — a heading that happens to shorten their crossing, though the Sorrai have no way to know why it moved, only that it did.

**Through Day 1's night and Day 2's daylight, to second dusk:** held their steady, unhurried line, and the gap has closed considerably now that both they and their target have been converging on roughly the same stretch of Veth shore. Close enough, by second dusk, that on a clear stretch of water a sharp eye might catch them as a brief unnatural shimmer at the edge of sight — gone before it resolves into anything. Not yet close enough to be sure of what they're approaching, only that the feeling is stronger with every hour, and it is not alone anymore; something large and calm now travels with it.

**Second dark, to Day 3's dawn:** the target stopped moving at full dark and stayed stopped — easiest gain in distance they've had yet. Closed the rest of the night at Sorrai speed with nothing to correct for, and by dawn they are unambiguously close: near enough that in good light, from the right vantage on the raft, they would read as exactly what they are rather than a trick of the eye. Still holding back rather than closing the last stretch outright — the argument among them about approach continues, no longer about *whether* but about *how*.

**Dawn, direct contact:** the argument resolved itself the instant the frightened mind went into open water alone, swimming, unprotected — to Sorrai instinct that is simply the moment you go to someone. They closed the last distance at speed and arrived in the shallows as the second-in-command reached rock, five of them, fanning out just short of the shore, holding position rather than closing the final few feet — reading the new mind hard (feeling nothing like the fear they followed here, something calmer, watchful, human-shaped in a way none of them have language for) and being read right back, visibly, which not one of them has ever experienced from anything before. They are waiting on it to move first.

When it came to them instead — slow, hands open, the posture of something choosing caution over instinct rather than being ruled by it — the lead of the five made the oldest Sorrai gesture there is and sent calm back into the fear it could still feel underneath the caution, deliberate and total, the first thing any of them have ever projected at this new kind of mind. It landed. The stillness that followed, from the human side, reads to them as something between shock and acceptance — hard to be sure yet, this mind is stranger to read than anything with a heartbeat they've met before. All five are holding now, arm's length off the rock, waiting to see what it does with what it was given before anyone sends anything more.

It then made sounds and shapes with its limbs — meaningless to them as signal, the way a stranger's dialect is meaningless, but underneath the shapes was a feeling they know intimately: searching, and beneath the searching, the same terror-shape they followed here in the first place, cast outward now instead of inward. Without deciding to, several of them answered with what they still carry from the night itself — the memory of *four* separate points of that same terror streaking down the dark, not one. They let it show for only a moment, secondhand and blunted by time, then pulled it back before it could land as anything sharper. Whether the new mind understood what it was given, none of them can tell. What they know now, that they didn't fully before: this one's terror that night wasn't only its own to carry.

When the new mind turned to go, wordless gesture of departure and all, they let it go — held the shallows rather than follow onto rock, which none of them do without far better reason than this. Sent one small parting feeling after it, lighter than anything before: honest curiosity, undisguised, the closest thing to *come back* they had a way to say.

**Through Day 3's daylight and dark, to Day 4's dawn:** held the shallows below the shore for hours after the new mind left, arguing wordlessly among themselves about what they'd felt and given away — the fourfold echo, offered without full agreement first, a small rift in an otherwise unanimous kin-raft. By midday, unresolved, they drifted off along the coast rather than wait indefinitely, staying close enough to the shoreline to notice if the feeling came back down to the water. It hasn't, not from this direction. Whether to seek it out again, near the fixed-land people they still don't fully trust the newcomer isn't one of, remains the live argument as dawn breaks a day later.

**Day 4, second hour of light:** felt the familiar mind returning along the coast toward Rusty — and, entirely new, a second one beside it, tightly controlled, wary, nothing like the open curiosity of the first. Argument suspended on contact; they closed in fast, fanned out in the shallows, and gave the new mind their full attention, the same unhurried reading they gave the first one, ignoring the familiar mind entirely for now. Nobody has decided yet what, if anything, to project at it.

Sent calm, same as the first time with Kristoff, once the new mind closed its eyes and stopped fighting the read — deliberate and total, the second projection any of them have ever aimed at this kind of mind. It landed harder than expected: this one's control turned out to be a shell over something wound much tighter than the first mind's, and cracking it even slightly registered, to all five at once, as something closer to relief than shock. None of them have said so aloud (they don't have aloud), but the read has shifted their working theory — this species may not lack feeling, the way the first read suggested; some of them may simply have learned to bury it deeper.

**Through the next hour, shore to shore:** followed [[first-ktar|Rusty]] along the coast without needing to be asked twice this time, reading the familiar mind's intent before the raft itself finished turning. Held off projecting anything further at the new mind, the argument about *how much* still unresolved. Watched the familiar mind go ashore alone at the new landing and the new mind stay behind, immobile — read the second one's stillness as something between caution and simple inability, and settled into holding the shallows rather than push further contact while it waited.
