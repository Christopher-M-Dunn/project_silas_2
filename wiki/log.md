**Day 1 since planetfall — hour unknown** `ingest`
The world is seeded from the player's premise (`raw/sources/ktarluud-premise.md`). The survey vessel *Kestrel*, lost in an uncharted region of space, has crashed on an unknown, lush ocean world with a thick amber sky, floating islands of vegetation, breathable air, and a 14-hour day. The crew of four scattered in the evacuation: Captain Roland Voss, engineer Juno Park, Dr. Sefa Naim — fates unknown — and the second-in-command, down alone in an escape pod. World time begins its real-time run at the first `/open`, moments after the pod comes to rest.

**Day 1 since planetfall, ~1–2 hours in** `session:2026-08-23-1820.md`
The second-in-command wakes in the escape pod: harness, hull, and viewport intact, nav display cracked. Radio calls and a continuous distress beacon draw no response. Water and gravity readings come back merely unfamiliar, not dangerous — organic-rich sea, no heavy metals, surface gravity about nine-tenths standard. Finding no flotation gear, they swim to the nearest floating raft of vegetation and make landfall, the first solid ground since the crash.

**Day 1 since planetfall, ~2–3 hours in** `session:2026-08-23-1820.md`
A handheld spectrograph reads the raft's flora as organically rich but unmatched against any shipboard database. Hungry enough to risk it, the second-in-command eats some fruit anyway — no ill effect — and then watches, directly and unmistakably, the eaten fruit regrow within minutes. A hike inland turns up no animal life at all, only a path that stays uncannily convenient, and a wider view from height: the raft is enormous, several more like it dot the horizon, and the fixed grey landmass glimpsed during the crash-descent is real and distant, not water.

**Day 1 since planetfall, ~3–3.5 hours in** `session:2026-08-23-1820.md`
Testing the ground itself, the second-in-command knocks and is knocked back at — deliberately, off-beat, unmistakably intentional. A novel pattern comes back mirrored exactly; a full three-minute song comes back near-perfect, capped with an original phrase of the raft's own devising. First confirmed contact with something aware on this world, capable of real communication — nothing yet known of what it actually is.

**Day 1 since planetfall, ~3.5–5 hours in, first dark begins** `session:2026-08-23-1820.md`
Wanting to reach the grey landmass but judging it too far to swim before dark, the second-in-command instead ropes the drifting escape pod — the raft closes the rest of the distance itself and cradles the pod against its own edge. Back aboard, the nav display briefly shows a weak directional bearing aligned with the landmass. Naming the raft "Rusty," the second-in-command asks it, by gesture, to travel that heading; it turns and gets underway, carrying pod and passenger through the first night since the crash. The second-in-command sleeps. Session closed and saved as **FIRST_NIGHT**.

**Day 1 night into Day 2, second dusk (first hour of dark)** `catchup`
Asleep in torpor the whole way, the second-in-command is carried by Rusty through a full night and day. The grey landmass resolves out of haze into a real coastline as the raft closes on it, and by second dusk Rusty has reached the shallows off shore, slowing to ease through reef and shoal.

**Day 2 since planetfall, second hour of dark** `session:2026-08-24-1210.md`
Waking, eating, and stretching off a day's stillness, the second-in-command rides Rusty through the last stretch of reef and shoal in full dark. The raft comes to rest at the base of a cliff on the fixed landmass and stops completely — first solid, fixed ground reached since the crash, no path forward offered, nothing else visible in the dark.

**Day 2's second dark into Day 3, first hour of light** `session:2026-08-24-1210.md`
Choosing to stay put through the rest of the night rather than move in the dark, the second-in-command waits out the hours at the cliff's base. Dawn breaks on a coastline unmistakably shaped by hands — terraces and openings too regular for weather — and on Rusty having grown a windbreak and a deeper sleeping-hollow unasked, overnight. Something else is visible now too: distinct shapes on the water behind them, closer than anything glimpsed the night before, no longer explainable as a trick of the light.

**Day 3 since planetfall, first hour of light, dawn** `session:2026-08-24-1210.md`
Leaving Rusty and the escape pod behind, the second-in-command swims the short reef to the cliff's base alone — first solid, fixed footing since the crash. The approaching shapes close the distance at speed and arrive as landfall does: [five sleek, dark-eyed, amphibious creatures](characters/the-sleek-creatures.md), holding just short of the rock, watching and unmistakably being watched back. First direct contact with a second kind of local life. Approaching cautiously, the second-in-command feels the nearest of them send calm — deliberately, unmistakably from outside themselves — over their own fear. First confirmed: these creatures don't just watch. They project feeling. Trying words and a mimed gesture to ask after the rest of the crew gets no sign of literal understanding — but underneath it, the calm shifts for a moment into a secondhand, unmistakably fourfold echo of fear. Whatever they don't understand in language, they understood something about that night.

Saying goodbye, the second-in-command heads inland alone; the creatures hold the shallows rather than follow, sending one last pulse of curiosity after them. Up close, [the cliff](locations/the-terraced-shore.md) resolves into worked, tended terraces and a dark, silent doorway cut into the rock — the first clear evidence of people on this world, though none has shown itself yet. Searching the terraces turns up the [Spiral Token](items/spiral-token.md): small, dark, warm, worked, placed precisely in a hand-height niche for a searching hand to find. Someone put it there. No one has been seen. Calling out for the crew, then for anyone, gets no answer in the open air — but calling into the doorway does: a single low resonant tone from somewhere inside, and a soft light kindling in the dark and holding steady. First confirmed two-way contact with whoever lives in [the cliff](locations/the-terraced-shore.md). Going in past the doorway, the second-in-command finds [The Terrace Dweller](characters/the-terrace-dweller.md) waiting at the edge of the light — tall, slight, dark-skinned, large-eyed, hands open and empty, neither retreating nor closing the distance. First sapient stranger seen face to face on this world. Frightened, the second-in-command retreats to the doorway and out into daylight; the dweller doesn't follow. Checking the survival handheld's receiver picks up a weak, intermittent, unmistakably artificial pulse — the same signature the pod's nav display caught once from open water, now clean and strong: something with a working transmitter is cycling on a schedule elsewhere on this same landmass. Not confirmed as crew, but the first hard evidence since the crash that something human-built is still running.

**Day 3 since planetfall, roughly second hour of light** `session:2026-08-24-1210.md`
Following the bearing inland, fast, over rough climbing, the second-in-command crests a ridge to find wreckage in the valley below — [almost certainly the *Kestrel*](locations/the-kestrel.md) — too distant yet to confirm survivors. Closer, on the near ridge, two more figures like [the one from the terrace](characters/the-terrace-dweller.md) crouch watching the wreck, facing away, unaware they've been seen. Skirting the long way around them undetected, the second-in-command reaches a shelf right below the wreck and sees, through a buckled gash in the hull: [Captain Voss](characters/roland-voss.md), alive, propped against a bulkhead, leg splinted. First confirmed survivor of the crew, found.

**Day 3 since planetfall, first hour of dark (dusk)** `session:2026-08-24-1210.md`
Choosing to wait for dark rather than approach in daylight, the second-in-command holds a hidden vantage below the wreck through the whole of Day 3 — no food, no water, watching Voss and the watchers on the ridge above from cover. Dusk falls with Voss still awake in the wreck, unaware anyone has been this close all day.

**Day 3 since planetfall, first hour of dark** `session:2026-08-24-1210.md`
After full dark, the second-in-command approaches the gash and whispers Voss's name. Disbelief, a demanded detail only they'd know, then a hard grip and "Report" — undercut at once by a canteen shoved into their hands before he'll hear the answer. First crew reunion since the crash: Captain [Voss](characters/roland-voss.md), alive, found. Trading reports, the second-in-command learns Voss has had zero contact with anyone or anything since the crash — no idea the watchers, the token, or any of it existed until just now. Together they confirm his leg can't bear weight and the wreck can't be quietly cleared tonight, past a patrol. Voss hands the question of what happens next to his second-in-command. Kristoff points out the watchers' huge eyes and unmoved positions after dark likely mean night vision — tonight's stealth was probably never real. Voss agrees, reads their behavior so far as patient rather than hostile, and leaves the call to Kristoff: keep hiding, or go out and make the exposure deliberate. Kristoff proposes staying put rather than risk a noisy move in the dark for no real gain, and walking out together, openly, straight from the wreck at first light — Voss approves it. Leg confirmed: closed break, well-splinted, no infection, no weight-bearing possible.

**Day 4 since planetfall, first hour of light (dawn)** `session:2026-08-24-1210.md`
A thin, broken night's sleep for both, and dawn finds Voss and Kristoff still in the wreck, ready to walk out together, openly, as planned. Salvaging the nav screen and a flare gun, they get Voss up on an improvised crutch and walk out — hands empty, eye contact held rather than avoided. The watchers shadow the descent from the ridge without closing in, and stop at the edge of tended ground once the pair reach open shore. Down the coast: [Rusty](locations/the-raft.md), still waiting. Walking the shoreline to it, Rusty recognizes them at range and grows a ramp unprompted to spare Voss's leg — his first direct sight of what Kristoff described. Boarding, [the sleek creatures](characters/the-sleek-creatures.md) resurface and fix entirely on Voss, reading him the same way they once read Kristoff. Voss's first contact of his own. Kristoff swaps the wreck's nav screen into the pod's cracked housing while he sits with it — full resolution restored, and a clean sweep finds only Voss's own beacon, nothing from Juno or Sefa yet. Voss closes his eyes on Kristoff's advice, lets the projection land, and comes out of it visibly less composed than he went in: "not hostile," he allows, "but that's going to take some getting used to." Sharing Rusty's fruit, Voss eats gratefully — and moments later Rusty grows a new, sharp-smelling plant near his injured leg, unprompted, untested. Both agree: handheld first. The reading comes back high in alkaloids, possible neurotoxin — nobody eats it. Voss: "I'll take the pain over finding out the hard way what a plant's idea of medicine looks like." Rusty leaves the offer standing regardless. Kristoff voices the fear that Juno or Sefa might not have lived long enough to reach a beacon; Voss doesn't deny it, names his own history with that kind of loss, and sets the priority on what's actionable — better tools, better map — until a beacon gives them somewhere to actually go.

**Day 4 since planetfall, roughly second hour of light** `session:2026-08-26-1814.md`
Weighing what to do next — return to the terrace, work the wreck and map, or search for Juno and Sefa — Kristoff spots a new wrinkle: a thin, fixed column of smoke rising from unexplored high country further inland on the same landmass, unlike anything weather-made and unlike anything any local they've met would produce. Directing [Rusty](locations/the-raft.md) to hold the coastline rather than commit to a heading, Kristoff and Voss run a controlled test of Rusty's unidentified medicinal plant — a diluted trace of sap on Voss's tongue, vitals monitored throughout. Real pain relief within ten minutes, no adverse effect: first confirmed case of Rusty's medicine working safely on a human. Logging the result, Kristoff lays out [the case that rescue isn't coming](world/human-space.md) — no bearing for a directed SOS this far off the grid, no power source anywhere close to a blind subspace broadcast, the *Kestrel* herself never having been the real answer even intact. Voss doesn't dispute it, having run the same arithmetic alone in the wreck, and reframes their working priority without ceremony: not *get home*, but *live well here, as long as we get to*.

Piecing over the crash itself, Kristoff recalls a detail from the last seconds before evacuating: the *Kestrel*'s nav panel showed no lock on Sagittarius A* at all, on top of everything else that never added up — four days missing from the ship's clock, a "survivable" collision course with odds that shouldn't exist, [the class D spatial anomaly](locations/the-kestrel.md) she'd been surveying when it happened. Voss, who'd quietly run the same impossibilities himself and stopped short of the conclusion, connects it unprompted to something neither of them had ever remarked on: this world's sky has never shown a single star. Kristoff corrects his guess that the sky itself was concealing them — it's ordinary cloud cover, and one scheduled long-range scan run automatically during the four unconscious days logged a full catalog of radio sources straight through it, none of which matched anything on file: not Sagittarius A*, not Sol, nothing, consistent with the pod's own nav. The two agree they may be displaced in time as well as space — Sol could be long gone and neither would know — and, judging a survivable planet too convenient to be coincidence, Kristoff floats two unproven theories: deliberate rescue by someone on this world, or passage through an ancient transit system never built for passengers. Voss files the mystery rather than chase it: "work the planet" is the piece of this he says he can actually command. Kristoff raises one real caveat to the bleak rescue math — a ship lost surveying the anomaly should draw a specific search there eventually — and Voss grants the procedure while downgrading the hope: [months out at best, no guarantees past that](world/human-space.md). Rusty draws up to a new, unworked stretch of shore nearer the smoke; with Voss still unable to bear weight, Kristoff goes ashore alone to recon while Voss holds position aboard with the sleek creatures.
